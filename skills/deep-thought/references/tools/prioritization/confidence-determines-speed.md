# Confidence determines speed

**Job.** Choose speed, quality, or a balance of the two from your confidence in the problem and in the solution.

**Not this.** A slogan for shipping sloppy work when being wrong is irreversible (data loss, safety, a migration you cannot undo). That cost is a constraint on the slice, not this model's axis. Confidence has to rest on evidence you can point at, not on a mood.

**Diagram.** Two judgments: confidence the problem matters, and confidence this solution is the right one. One of the three paces written under them.

**Steps.**
1. Separate the two judgments. How confident are you that the problem is worth solving? How confident are you that this solution is the right one?
2. Low confidence that the problem matters: favor speed. Shrink the slice so the next loop can raise that confidence.
3. High confidence in the problem and high confidence in the solution: favor quality. Name what quality means here (review, test, rollout) so it is not an open-ended delay.
4. High confidence in the problem and low confidence in the solution: balance speed and quality. Learn about the solution without polishing a guess.
5. Confidence is a scale. If you sit between two of these, say which way you are leaning and what evidence would move you.

**Source.** Brandon Chu, confidence as the speed-versus-quality tradeoff. Worksheet: [Untools](https://untools.co/confidence-determines-speed-vs-quality/).
