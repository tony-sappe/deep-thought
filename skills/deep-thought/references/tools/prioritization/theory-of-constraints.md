# Theory of constraints

**Job.** Find the bottleneck and subordinate the rest of the system to it.

**Not this.** A personal urgency sort (Eisenhower) or a pile of independent tasks (impact–effort). If improving a non-constraint feels productive, that feeling is the trap this tool exists to catch.

**Diagram.** The flow of work, one constraint marked, every other step labeled subordinate or not yet.

**Steps.**
1. Name the system's goal in throughput terms: what "done" means per unit of time.
2. Identify the constraint. The constraint is the resource or policy where work piles up or where the goal is limited. One constraint, not a tie you are unwilling to break.
3. Exploit it. Get more of the goal out of the constraint without buying more of it: cut its idle time, cut work that arrives half-ready, stop using it on non-goal work.
4. Subordinate. Change upstream and downstream steps so they serve the constraint's pace. A local efficiency that feeds the constraint faster than it can take work is not a win.
5. Elevate only if exploit and subordinate are not enough. Add capacity at the constraint, then go back to step 2. The constraint will move. Do not keep optimizing the old one.

**Source.** Eliyahu Goldratt, the theory of constraints.
