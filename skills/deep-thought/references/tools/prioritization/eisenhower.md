# Eisenhower matrix

**Job.** Sort work by urgency and importance, then do, schedule, delegate, or drop it.

**Not this.** The question is value versus cost of doing (impact–effort), or a system bottleneck (theory of constraints). Urgent is a time claim. Important is a consequence claim. Do not let the loud item define both.

**Diagram.** Four quadrants. Urgent/not urgent across the top. Important/not important down the side.

**Steps.**
1. List the items competing for this person's time. Keep the list to what is actually on their plate.
2. Urgent: a real deadline or a real cost of waiting. Important: it changes an outcome they are accountable for. Place each item.
3. Important and urgent: do it, and ask why it became urgent.
4. Important and not urgent: schedule it. This quadrant is the point of the tool. Name the calendar slot.
5. Urgent and not important: delegate or shrink it. Not important and not urgent: drop it. A quadrant with no drops is a mood, not a sort.

**Source.** The urgent-versus-important distinction is attributed to Dwight D. Eisenhower. The four quadrants and the actions do, schedule, delegate, and drop are Stephen Covey's matrix in *The 7 Habits of Highly Effective People*. [Untools](https://untools.co) is a worksheet.
