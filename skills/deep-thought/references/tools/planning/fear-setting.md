# Fear-setting

**Job.** Price the downside of acting, how you would prevent or repair it, and the cost of leaving the fear in charge.

**Not this.** A premortem of a plan you have already chosen, or inversion of a goal. Fear-setting is for a decision stuck on anxiety. It is not therapy, and it is not a dare to ignore a real safety constraint.

**Diagram.** Three columns: define (what could go wrong), prevent / repair, and the cost of inaction.

**Steps.**
1. Define. List the specific bad outcomes you are avoiding by not acting. Vague dread gets rewritten until it is a scene.
2. Prevent. For each outcome, name the action that lowers its chance before you start.
3. Repair. If it happens anyway, what do you do in the first week? If an outcome cannot be repaired and you cannot accept it, that item is a constraint, not a fear to push through.
4. Cost of inaction. Write what the next six months and the next three years look like if you do not act. Include partial, boring costs.
5. Choose with those prices visible. The tool ends in a decision or a named constraint, not in a braver mood.

**Source.** Tim Ferriss, the fear-setting worksheet: define the downside, prevent it, repair it, and price inaction. Stoic premeditation of evils is the older root, not this grid. Ryan Holiday and Ness Labs are indexes.
