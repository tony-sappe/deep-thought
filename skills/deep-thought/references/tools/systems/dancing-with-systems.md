# Dancing with systems

**Job.** After a model exists, check the stance of the intervention against Meadows' practices. The work is to dance with the system, not to control it.

**Not this.** You do not yet have a picture of behavior over time. Get the beat before you adopt a stance. This page is not a substitute for a loop, a stock, or a leverage rank.

**Diagram.** The proposed intervention beside the one practice it violates. Not a poster of all fourteen.

The practices, in the essay's order:

1. Get the beat.
2. Listen to the wisdom of the system.
3. Expose your mental models to the open air.
4. Stay humble. Stay a learner.
5. Honor and protect information.
6. Locate responsibility in the system.
7. Make feedback policies for feedback systems.
8. Pay attention to what is important, not just what is quantifiable.
9. Go for the good of the whole.
10. Expand time horizons.
11. Expand thought horizons.
12. Expand the boundary of caring.
13. Celebrate complexity.
14. Hold fast to the goal of goodness.

**Steps.**
1. State the intervention you are about to make.
2. Get the beat in one line: the behavior over time, not the absence of your favorite solution.
3. Pick the single practice the intervention most clearly violates. Ignore the rest in this pass.
4. Change the intervention so it respects that practice. Typical repairs: watch longer, publish the model, return the feedback to the decision maker, lengthen the time horizon, or stop optimizing a part.
5. Do not add a control layer to remove surprise. The essay's claim is that these systems are not controllable that way.

**Source.** Donella Meadows, [Dancing with Systems](https://donellameadows.org/archives/dancing-with-systems/). The essay lists these fourteen practices. Do not invent a fifteenth.
