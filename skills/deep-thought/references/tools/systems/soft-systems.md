# Soft systems methodology

**Job.** Work a messy human situation where people do not agree what the system is, by naming worldviews and comparing purposeful models to what is actually happening.

**Not this.** The system is a machine with an agreed purpose and you need stocks, flows, and delays (system dynamics). Also not an org chart (viable system model) and not only a boundary critique (critical systems heuristics).

**Diagram.** A rich picture of the mess, then one root definition built with CATWOE, then a conceptual model compared with the picture.

**Steps.**
1. Draw the situation before you name a system. Include people, conflict, and what they care about. Use the rich-picture page if the drawing is the whole next move.
2. For one relevant way of seeing it, fill CATWOE: who benefits or suffers (customers), who acts (actors), what transforms into what (transformation), the worldview that makes this transformation meaningful, who could stop it (owner), and the constraints taken as given (environment).
3. Write a root definition: a system to do the transformation, by means of the actors, in order to serve those customers, under that worldview.
4. List the activities a system of that definition would have to contain. This is a conceptual model, not a description of the office.
5. Compare the model to the picture. Differences are the debate, not yet the plan.
6. Name only changes that are both desirable under the worldview and culturally feasible. Do not smuggle in a worldview nobody in the situation holds.

**Source.** Peter Checkland, soft systems methodology. CATWOE is the root-definition checklist used inside it. The six moves here compress the usual SSM cycle. They are not a transcript of Checkland's seven stages. OpenLearn is orientation.
