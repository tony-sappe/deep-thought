# Critical systems heuristics

**Job.** Critique the boundary of the system: who is in, who is out, and whose view counts as expertise.

**Not this.** You need a model of flows (system dynamics) or an agreed transformation (soft systems root definition) and the boundary is not the dispute. This page does not itself redesign the process.

**Diagram.** Four boundary issues, each with an "is" and an "ought": motivation, control, knowledge, legitimacy.

**Steps.**
1. State the boundary currently in force. Who does the plan treat as inside the system?
2. Motivation: who benefits, and what is the purpose? Who ought to benefit?
3. Control: who decides, and what resources do they command? Who ought to decide?
4. Knowledge: whose expertise counts, and what is the guarantor of that knowledge? What ought to count?
5. Legitimacy: who is a witness for those affected but not involved? Who ought to be able to challenge the boundary?
6. Record the gaps between is and ought. A gap is the output. Do not paper it over with a new org chart in the same pass.

**Source.** Werner Ulrich, critical systems heuristics. Orientation: [OpenLearn systems thinking](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/systems-thinking-hub).
