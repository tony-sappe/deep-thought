# Stock and flow

**Job.** Separate what accumulates from the rates that fill and drain it, and name the delays between them.

**Not this.** You only need the sign of a loop and not the accumulation. Use a causal loop diagram. A stock is not a goal and not an event.

**Diagram.** A bathtub. The stock is the level. Inflows and outflows are the faucets and drains. Delays marked on the pipes.

**Steps.**
1. Name the stock: the accumulation you could measure if time stopped (backlog, cash, trust, defects, skill, inventory).
2. Name the inflows and the outflows. A stock does not change except through its flows.
3. Mark delays between a decision and a flow, and between a flow and the level people watch.
4. Sketch the level over time that you have actually seen. A single incident is not a graph.
5. Find which loop drives the flows before anyone proposes a new target number for the stock.

**Source.** Donella Meadows, *Thinking in Systems* (the bathtub). Jay Forrester and John Sterman, system dynamics.
