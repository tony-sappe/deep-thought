# Limits to growth

**Job.** Find the reinforcing success that is about to hit a constraint, and the balancing loop that constraint creates.

**Not this.** There is no reinforcing success. A single balancing loop seeking a stable goal is not this archetype. Pushing the reinforcing loop harder is the usual low-leverage mistake.

**Diagram.** A reinforcing loop, a limiting stock or capacity, and a balancing loop that strengthens as the limit is approached. Behavior over time: rise, then slow, stall, or collapse.

**Steps.**
1. Name the reinforcing loop that has been producing the growth or the success.
2. Name the stock or capacity that success consumes, fills, or erodes.
3. Draw the balancing loop that gets stronger as that limit is hit. Include the delay if the limit is noticed late.
4. Ask whether the proposed fix pushes the reinforcing loop (more effort) or relaxes the constraint.
5. Name an early signal that the limit is near, taken from the limiting stock, not from the success metric alone.

**Source.** Peter Senge, *The Fifth Discipline*, the limits-to-growth archetype: a reinforcing success meets a balancing constraint. Donella Meadows, Dennis Meadows, Jørgen Randers, and William Behrens, *The Limits to Growth*, is the World3 study, not this diagram. This page teaches the archetype.
