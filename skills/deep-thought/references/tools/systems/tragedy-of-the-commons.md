# Tragedy of the commons

**Job.** Find a shared stock that individuals exploit because the gain is private and the cost is delayed or spread.

**Not this.** One actor, one decision, no shared stock. Also not a motive problem (Hanlon's razor). Restraint speeches are not the intervention. The structure is the missing or delayed feedback to the user.

**Diagram.** One shared stock. Two or more users. Each user has a reinforcing gain. The stock's decline feeds back weakly or late to each user.

**Steps.**
1. Name the shared stock in units you could measure.
2. Name the users who gain privately by taking from it or by adding to its load.
3. Draw each user's gain, and the feedback from the stock's condition back to that user. If that feedback is immediate and personal, this is not a commons.
4. Check that the cost lands on the stock or on everyone, not on the user in proportion to their take.
5. Name an intervention at information, rules, or who feels the cost. If you need the rank of that move, use leverage points as a complement.

**Source.** Garrett Hardin, "The Tragedy of the Commons" (*Science*, 1968), is the essay. The diagram on this page is the systems archetype as taught by Peter Senge in *The Fifth Discipline* and discussed by Donella Meadows: a shared stock, private gain, and weak or delayed feedback to the user. The page teaches that diagram, not Hardin's population argument.
