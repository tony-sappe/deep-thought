# Iceberg

**Job.** Place the event at the layer that actually produces it: event, pattern, structure, mental model, or goal and paradigm.

**Not this.** You already know the layer and need the loop, the stock, or the intervention. The iceberg places the work. It is not the intervention list (leverage points) and not one person's leap (ladder of inference).

**Diagram.** Five layers, shallow on top. Put the current story on the layer it belongs to, and mark the deeper layer you have evidence for.

**Steps.**
1. Events. Write the occurrence as something that happened once, with a time.
2. Patterns. What has repeated? If you cannot say, you do not have a pattern yet. Get the history before you go deeper.
3. Structure. What stocks, rules, or information flows make that pattern likely? Stay with arrangement, not with blame.
4. Mental models. What belief makes that structure feel reasonable to the people inside it?
5. Goals/paradigm. What is the system for, and what worldview makes that goal obvious?
6. Work at the deepest layer you can support with evidence. Name the layer you are explicitly not working at yet.

**Source.** The systems-thinking iceberg: events, patterns of behavior, systemic structure, and mental models. Michael Goodman’s account in *The Systems Thinker* is a standard statement of those four layers. Goals/paradigm is this guide’s fifth label, so the page matches the router. Donella Meadows’ intervention list is the leverage-points page.
