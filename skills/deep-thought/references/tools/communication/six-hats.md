# Six thinking hats

**Job.** Run a meeting so everyone takes the same kind of thinking at the same time.

**Not this.** A personality label ("she's a black hat"). Hats are modes, worn in sequence, by the whole group. Also not a way to avoid a decision that already has an owner.

**Diagram.** The sequence of hats you will actually use, with a time box. Blue opens and closes.

**Steps.**
1. Blue. State the decision or the question, and the sequence. Blue is process, not content.
2. White. Facts and gaps only. What do we know, what is missing, how would we know.
3. Use the hats the question needs, in an order you announce. Red: feelings, said briefly, not justified. Black: risks and why it might fail. Yellow: value and why it might work. Green: alternatives, after black and yellow have been heard.
4. One hat at a time. A black-hat objection during green gets parked until black.
5. Blue closes. What did we decide, what is the next action, which hat we still owe if the sequence was cut short.

**Source.** Edward de Bono, six thinking hats. Worksheet form: [Untools](https://untools.co).
