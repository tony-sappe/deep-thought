# What makes you say that?

**Job.** Force the evidence under an interpretation, in the moment, with two questions.

**Not this.** A written claim–support–question when you are editing a document. This page is the spoken routine. It is not an interrogation. Ask it of your own conclusion too.

**Diagram.** Two lines. "What is going on?" and "What do you see that makes you say that?"

**Steps.**
1. When someone states an interpretation, ask what they think is going on. Let them say the interpretation plainly.
2. Ask what they see, hear, or read that makes them say that. Stay with evidence. "Because I feel it" is a start, not a finish.
3. If they offer more interpretation, return to what in the thing itself they are pointing at.
4. Paraphrase the evidence back. Check that you heard the evidence, not a straw man.
5. Only then add what you see. The routine fails if the asker skips their own evidence.

**Source.** Harvard Project Zero, What Makes You Say That? [Visible thinking routines](https://pz.harvard.edu/thinking-routines).
