# Minto pyramid

**Job.** Lead with the answer, then group the support so a reader can stop at any level and still have the point.

**Not this.** A way to discover the answer. Discover it with the tool the problem needs, then use this page to say it. A pyramid with the reasoning on top and the answer at the bottom is an essay, not this tool.

**Diagram.** One governing sentence. Under it, two to four grouped supports. Under each support, the facts that belong only to it.

**Steps.**
1. Write the situation the reader already accepts, the complication that creates a question, and the question.
2. Answer the question in one sentence. That sentence is the top of the pyramid. A reader who stops there must have the point.
3. Group the support into a few lines that are the same kind of thing (reasons, or steps, or parts). The groups must not overlap, and together they must justify the answer.
4. Order the groups. Time order for a process, structure order for parts, importance order for reasons.
5. Put evidence under the group it supports. Cut any fact that does not serve the governing sentence.

**Source.** Barbara Minto, *The Pyramid Principle*. Worksheet form: [Untools](https://untools.co).
