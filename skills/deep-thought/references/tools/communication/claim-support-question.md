# Claim–support–question

**Job.** Pressure-test an assertion by separating the claim, the support, and the question that remains.

**Not this.** A search for a root cause, or a debate to win. If there is no support, the output is "unsupported", not a louder claim. Use "What makes you say that?" when you need the evidence pulled out of a person in the moment. Use this page when you are writing the assertion down.

**Diagram.** Three lines under the assertion: claim, support, question.

**Steps.**
1. Claim: the assertion in one sentence, strong enough to be wrong.
2. Support: the facts or reasons offered for it. Mark which are observed and which are appeals to authority or analogy.
3. Question: what is still unresolved if the support is true? A good question is one the claim has not answered.
4. If the support does not reach the claim, say so and stop. Do not fill the gap with a plausible story.
5. Hand the remaining question to the tool that can answer it, or leave it open on purpose.

**Source.** Harvard Project Zero, Claim Support Question. [Visible thinking routines](https://pz.harvard.edu/thinking-routines).
