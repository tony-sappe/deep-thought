# Hanlon's razor

**Job.** Prefer mistake, overload, or a local incentive over malice when both explain the same observations.

**Not this.** You already have evidence of intent. Or the harm is produced by a structure (a commons, a rule, a loop) that does not need a motive. Do not use this page to excuse a repeated structural failure.

**Diagram.** Observations in the middle. A malice story on one side, a non-malicious cause on the other, each tied only to observations that support it.

**Steps.**
1. State the harmful behavior without a motive in the sentence.
2. List what was observed. Separate that list from the motive already inferred.
3. Write a non-malicious cause that fits the observations: ignorance, overload, a bad interface, a local incentive, a missing fact.
4. Ask what evidence would still require intent. If you do not have it, proceed on the non-malicious cause.
5. Choose the fix for that cause. Do not design a punishment for a motive you have not shown.

**Source.** Robert J. Hanlon, the line published in Arthur Bloch's *Murphy's Law Book Two: More Reasons Why Things Go Wrong* (1980): do not attribute to malice what is adequately explained by stupidity. The two-column evidence procedure is this page's worksheet. Farnam Street is an index.
