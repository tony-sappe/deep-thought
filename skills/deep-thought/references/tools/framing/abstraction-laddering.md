# Abstraction laddering

**Job.** Move the problem up (why) and down (how) until it sits at the altitude of the decision.

**Not this.** You need truths that remain if the current design disappears (first principles), or a MECE split of a question you already trust (issue tree).

**Diagram.** A vertical ladder. Mark the rung where the problem is now, and the rung where the decision sits.

**Steps.**
1. Write the problem in the words already in use. That is the current rung.
2. Ask why. Each answer is a higher rung. Stop when a further why would change who owns the problem or what success means.
3. Ask how. Each answer is a lower rung. Stop when you can name an action a specific person can take.
4. Choose the rung the next decision actually requires. Rewrite the problem in one sentence at that altitude.
5. Name one rung you will not work at in this pass.

**Source.** S. I. Hayakawa, the ladder of abstraction. Worksheet form: [Untools](https://untools.co).
