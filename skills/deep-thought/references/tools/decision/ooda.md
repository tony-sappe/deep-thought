# OODA

**Job.** Cycle Observe, Orient, Decide, Act fast enough to act under incomplete data. Orientation does the work. Speed without a new observation is thrash.

**Not this.** Criteria are shared and the facts are sufficient for a matrix. Also not a license to skip orientation and call impatience a loop.

**Diagram.** A cycle. Orientation in the center, fed by what was just observed and by what the last action changed.

**Steps.**
1. Observe. What is different since the last cycle? Separate observation from the story already in use.
2. Orient. What mental model, cultural habit, or previous result is interpreting those observations? Name one assumption orientation is relying on.
3. Decide. The smallest decision that the current orientation can support. A decision that requires certainty you do not have is a refusal to cycle.
4. Act. Take that action so the next observation has something to see.
5. Say what you expect to observe next, and how soon you will re-enter the loop. Implicit guidance is allowed when the team already shares the orientation. Say so, instead of pretending a long decision memo happened.

**Source.** John Boyd, the OODA loop. Observe, orient, decide, act, with orientation doing the work, is his. The five steps here are one pass through a cycle, not his full account of orientation. [Untools](https://untools.co) is a worksheet.
